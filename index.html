Design the swarm that would watch the whole solar system

This studio models a single idea: a standing crew of satellites around every interesting body in the solar system, keeping watch. It starts counting at 200 targets and 1,300 spacecraft, but no rule says those numbers: on the scale page the fleet total and the policy for spending it are dials in your hands, with the downstream numbers computed for you to weigh. Every page is a working model you can pull on.

The Sun, the eight planets and our Moon, each wearing humanity's real map of it: NASA Blue Marble, Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter, MESSENGER, Viking, Cassini and Voyager data. Bodies we have not mapped are drawn plain, in their measured colour, on the catalogue.

This is a design study, not a mission. Nothing on this site has been built or launched. The maths is teaching-grade mechanics: right enough to compare options and plan shapes, not to fly a spacecraft. The about page lists every approximation.

The idea in one breath

At the starting setting: two hundred funded targets, about the number of countries on Earth, each with a standing crew: a baseline picket of four satellites, one over each pole and one over each side, rising to dense swarms of fifty at busy worlds and a hundred wrapped right around the Sun, so every side of our star is watched at once. Around the funded core rides the census: every named moon of the giant planets and a deeper sweep of small worlds, watched by the host swarms until the dial funds them too. At this setting the model builds about 1,300 spacecraft, and every one of them eventually wears out, which is why replacement gets its own pages.

The tools

The scale

The fleet total and the allocation policy as dials in your hands, with production, cost, coverage and traffic computed at every setting.

The catalogue

All 384 bodies: planets, every named giant-planet moon, asteroids, comets, ice worlds and three interstellar visitors. Search it, group it, argue with it.

Orbit map

Every heliocentric target on one animated 3D map, positioned by real orbital elements. Drag to tilt and spin, scrub time forwards and back.

Gravity lab

Gravity as a landscape: Lagrange points, tadpole and horseshoe orbits, and arrival insertions you brake by hand.

Orbit lab

Pick a body and design a stable orbit around it: how low is safe, how high is stable, and what a lap takes.

Launch windows

When the doors open, and how money bends the schedule: dial in extra push and watch every date move.

Satellite builder

Fit a bus with instruments and an engine, watch the budgets move, and see whether your design survives its destination.

The rides

Two fleets for two problems: chemical fire for the gravity well; ion, Neumann Drive, nuclear and sail for the road.

Instruments

The kit of parts: fifteen modular instruments, each explained in plain words, with the recommended fit-out for every target class.

Convoys

Nothing flies alone: batch missions ride-share to each system, then disperse. Deployment convoys, then renewal convoys on every window.

Network

Data relays, light lag and control: who decides what, at every distance, when a command takes hours to arrive.

Fleet

How 1,300 satellites spread across 200 targets. Drag the tier dials and watch the whole fleet re-total.

Replacement cadence

Satellites die. This page models how fast, and what the factory line would need to produce to keep every picket whole.

About

What this is, what it leans on, every approximation named, and the licence.

How a mission threads through

The tools chain together. Pick a target from the 200, and its dossier hands you onward: the next launch window to get there, a stable orbit to park in, a satellite build that suits the destination, and the replacement drumbeat that keeps its picket alive. Try Enceladus, Psyche or Sedna to see a full dossier.
