The scale

No hard rule says 200 targets, or four satellites to a picket, or 1,300 in the fleet. Those are starting points, the place a long idea began counting. This page treats every number in the study as one dial, solves the allocation at any setting, and holds the result against the two questions that decide whether a thing like this deserves to exist.

The two gates. One: it must not disrupt the solar system: no wreckage, no crowding, no contaminated oceans, no commons spoiled. Two: it must earn its keep: warnings that protect people, custody of the hazards, knowledge given freely, a door answered when the galaxy sends a visitor. A network that passes both gates at some scale has made its case to be built; one that cannot, has not.

      What this page is, honestly

      This is the highest-altitude, lowest-fidelity model on the site: proportional scaling laws pinned so the dial at 1,332 reproduces the starting design exactly, with priority-ranked promotion of the census as the budget grows. Its job is to make the shape of the question computable, not to settle it. The finer pages (fleet, cadence, convoys, network) currently run at the starting point; solving the whole system to a fidelity worth building is the actual project, and every model here is a rung toward it.

      The compass the study steers by: a network like this is worth building only if it empowers joyful, responsible abundance for humanity and its place in the galaxy, and harms nothing on the way. That is the standard both gates are trying to measure, in so many words.
